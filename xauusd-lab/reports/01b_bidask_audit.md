## 6. Measured cost model (replaces the assumed C0–C3 of Phase 1)

| Scenario | Definition |
|---|---|
| C0 | fill at the real Ask (buy) / Bid (sell); no commission, no slippage |
| C1 (base) | real Ask/Bid fill + $0.10 adverse slippage on market & stop orders (~mean |mid move| over 0.5-1 s), $0 on limit orders + broker commission (unknown, $0 until confirmed) |
| C2 (stress) | C1 with spread x2 and $0.30 slippage on market & stop orders (~p90 of 1 s mid move) |
| C3 (news) | C1 + extra $0.50 slippage within ±2 min of 15:30/17:00/21:00 server; no entries 23:55-01:05 server |
| 2022 → 2026-03 | Ask = Bid + (M1-file SPREAD, which equals the minute's minimum spread) + measured hour-of-day median markup (table ba_spread_markup_by_hour.csv). Spread is modelled in USD, not bps, because the $0.10 floor does not scale with price |

Measured median round-trip spread $0.104, p90 $0.129 (time-weighted, 2026 price levels).

Real spread minus the minimum spread recorded in the M1 file ("markup"), time-weighted: {'p50': 0.0042, 'p75': 0.0145, 'p90': 0.0284, 'p95': 0.0403, 'p99': 0.0753, 'p99.9': 0.1645}. Spearman(daily spread $, daily price) = 0.486: the dollar spread rises only weakly with price (monthly table: price +18% from Nov to Apr, spread +8%), while its *minimum* stays on a fixed $0.10 floor. The M1 file shows the same 10-point minimum back to 2022, at less than half today's price. Scaling the spread in bps would therefore understate 2022–2024 costs by more than half, so cost is modelled **in USD** for the whole history (the conservative choice).

By hour: `reports/tables/ba_spread_markup_by_hour.csv`.

## 7. Findings

1. **The two files agree perfectly.** Every one of the 219,742 overlapping minutes has identical Bid OHLC and identical tick volume. The M1 file is therefore a faithful Bid series built from these ticks, which raises confidence in the 2022–2026 history.
2. **The M1 file's SPREAD column is the minimum spread of the minute** (100% exact match). It systematically understates cost: the real maximum spread exceeded it in 81% of minutes.
3. **Normal cost is low and stable:** a round trip costs about $0.10 (median) to $0.13 (p90), roughly 0.25 bps of price. Hours 01–08 server (Asia) are ~10–15% wider than London/NY.
4. **The danger zone is the daily reopen.** At 01:00 server the median *maximum* spread is $4.00 and reached $38.71; stops resting over the halt can fill far away. Rule: no new entries 23:55–01:05 server, and positions are not held through the halt unless a strategy is explicitly designed for it.
5. **News minutes widen the spread less than feared** (15:30 median max spread $0.16, p90 $1.61), but price moves a lot, so the real news cost is slippage, not spread.
6. **Same-minute ambiguity is a coin flip.** When price touches ±X in both directions inside one minute, the high came first ≈50% of the time. "SL first" is therefore genuinely conservative, and it matters: at 2026 volatility, ±$1 is touched both ways in 7.7% of minutes. Scalping models with stops/targets of ~$1 or less must be validated on ticks, not on M1 bars.
7. **Latency is a real cost for scalping:** a 1-second delay moves the mid by $0.15 on average (p90 $0.37), comparable to the whole spread.

**Limitation:** 7.4 months only, all inside the holdout period. Used for cost calibration and simulator validation only, never for selecting strategy rules.
